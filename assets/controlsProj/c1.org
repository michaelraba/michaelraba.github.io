* The system
** What we're building
- *Ball on a 300 mm beam*, balanced by tilting the beam about a *center pivot*
- *Sensor:* VL53L1X time-of-flight (TOF400C board), measures ball position
- *Actuator:* hobby servo plus a linkage
- *Controller:* Arduino Mega 2560, driven from *Simulink*
- Goal: hold the ball at a setpoint and reject disturbances

** Why a center pivot
- Beam is balanced about its middle, so the servo only fights *friction and ball torque*, not beam weight
- Symmetric: same dynamics either direction
- Simple, linear-ish small-angle model
- Tradeoff: the linkage has to reach out to a point on the beam (130 mm from pivot)

* Part A.1: Nominal dynamics
** The model in one slide
For a solid sphere rolling without slipping:

\[ \ddot{x} = \frac{5}{7}\, g \sin\theta \]

Small angle, \( \sin\theta \approx \theta \):

\[ \frac{X(s)}{\Theta(s)} \approx \frac{(5/7)\,g}{s^{2}} \]

- A *double integrator*: no damping, no restoring force
- Open loop it is *unstable*, so we need feedback (PID)
- Servo treated as instantaneous: \( \theta = K \cdot \text{PWM} \)

** Where 5/7 comes from
- Energy: \( \tfrac12 m v^2 + \tfrac12 I \omega^2 \) with \( I = \tfrac25 m R^2 \), \( v = \omega R \)
- Gives \( \ddot{x} = \dfrac{g \sin\theta}{1 + I/(mR^2)} = \dfrac{g\sin\theta}{1.4} \)
- \( 1/1.4 = 5/7 \approx 0.714 \)

* Part A.2: Reality vs. nominal
** Where the real system differs
| Effect                      | Nominal | Real                          |
|-----------------------------+---------+-------------------------------|
| Ball on *two rails*         | 0.714 g sinθ | *0.638 g sinθ* (about 11% lower) |
| Servo gain                  | ideal   | unknown K, small deadband     |
| Linkage                     | linear  | about 10% non-linear over ±10° |
| Slop / backlash             | none    | joints and pivot               |
| Friction                    | none    | bearings, rolling resistance   |
| Sensor                      | exact   | noise, cone clips the stop     |

** Rails reduce the acceleration
- Ball sits on two rails, so it rolls on a *smaller effective radius*
- \( r_{eff} = R\sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{s/2}{R + r}\right)^2} = 10.65\ \text{mm} \)
- With R = 12.7 mm, rail radius 2 mm, spacing 16 mm

\[ \ddot{x} = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + 0.4\,(R/r_{eff})^2} = 0.638\, g\sin\theta \]

- The plant gain is about *11% lower* than nominal
- We can *measure* this and compare in Part B

** Linkage non-linearity
- Horn 32 mm, rod 33 mm, rod joint 130 mm out
- ±10° beam gives horn angles of roughly *-42.9° to +46.7°*
- About *10% asymmetry*, so beam angle is not exactly linear in command
- Fix: calibrate with a lookup or a fitted polynomial

** Backlash and slop
- Slop at the beam is the joint slop *divided by the lever ratio*
- Long beam lever (130 mm) vs. short horn (32 mm) helps us
- A *preload spring* keeps the joints loaded one way, so there's no rattle
- Plain bearings at the pivot instead of a printed hole

** The sensor has issues
- VL53L1X *minimum range about 40 mm*; our closest ball surface is *47 mm*. OK
- Field of view is a *27° cone*. It clips the end stop and rails
  - Plan: *narrow the ROI* in the sensor config
- Chrome ball may reflect poorly
  - Plan: matte tape, or test first
- *Check the TOF400C is 5 V tolerant* before wiring to 5 V

** Center pivot assembly
- M5 shoulder bolt, two MR106ZZ bearings (6x10x3), spacer sleeve, M5 nylon-lock nut
- Bearing choice: ordered *plain* MR106ZZ, so I redrew the hub for plain bearings
- They sit 0.8 mm proud of the hub faces
- Nylon-lock nut: tight enough to remove play, not tight enough to bind
